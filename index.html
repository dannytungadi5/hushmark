What is in the box

Reads the document

Photo or camera in, structured fields out: provider, reference, name, date, total, tax, every printed line.

Hybrid inference

Gemini Nano in Chrome through Firebase AI Logic when the browser has it. Otherwise Hybrid mode falls back to Gemini in the cloud. Private mode never does.

Date logic

Filing window, dated before the incident, outside the trip dates, in the future.

Caps per category

Editable limits, plus a room-per-day cap for health and a labour share for motor.

Exclusion lists

Cosmetic and vitamins, wear and servicing items, alcohol, gifts and souvenirs.

Duplicates and mismatches

Same bill twice, a different patient name or plate across the same claim.

Arithmetic

Lines plus tax against the printed total, and missing itemisation on large bills.

Note to the claimant

CSV and print

Why Hushmark, not another claims suite

1

The document stays put

Chrome's Prompt API runs Gemini Nano in the browser, which Google's docs say protects privacy, cuts server costs and works offline. Hushmark builds on that, so a claimant's bill is not another upload to review.

Source: developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/built-in-ai/language-model
2

Your rules decide, not a model's mood

The AI only reads. Caps, dates and exclusions are plain code a handler can read, edit and audit. Same document, same answer, every time.

3

One engine, four desks

Health, motor, travel and office expenses run on the same engine. A new desk is a rule set, not a new product.

Where should this document be read?

1

On the device

Where Chrome has built-in Gemini Nano, it reads the photo on the device, so a patient's bill or a number plate never leaves the phone or laptop. The Nano path follows the official Chrome API but is not yet tested on a real Nano device. The cloud fallback is tested.

2

Cloud, only if allowed

If Nano is missing and you chose Hybrid, Firebase AI Logic falls back to Gemini in the cloud, guarded by App Check. Every card says which path ran.

3

Rules, not guesses

Caps, exclusions, filing windows, incident and trip dates, duplicates and arithmetic are plain code. The AI reads and drafts a note. It never approves or denies a claim.

Who feels it, and what it costs

  • About 1 in 5 in-network claims were denied by HealthCare.gov plans in 2023: 56 million denied claims. Of the denials with a stated reason, 21% were administrative and 14% an excluded service; only 6% were medical necessity. Each denial is a round trip for the claimant and the handler. KFF, "Claims Denials and Appeals in ACA Marketplace Plans in 2023", kff.org/private-insurance/claims-denials-and-appeals-in-aca-marketplace-plans-in-2023
  • About 5% of health claims in Indonesia are estimated by the regulator OJK to involve fraud. Kontan, 13 Jun 2025: keuangan.kontan.co.id/news/ojk-perkirakan-fraud-asuransi-kesehatan-5-dari-total-klaim-ini-kata-pengamat
  • Users: first-line claims handlers at small insurers, TPAs and brokers, who open every photo by hand. Cost of a wrong call: a wrongly paid claim is leakage, a wrongly denied one is an appeal and an angry claimant. Hushmark does neither: it flags a missing name, a date before the accident or a duplicate so a person looks first.

Numbers are public and cited. We did not run a pilot and have no user quotes. What share of claims Hushmark would catch is unmeasured.

The problem

Claims arrive as photos and PDFs. Handlers open each one to find the same avoidable things: a missing patient name, an invoice dated before the accident, a wiper blade on a collision claim, the same bill sent twice. Most are errors, not fraud, and each costs a round trip. The share of claims with avoidable document errors is not validated by us.

Who pays

Claims teams at small and mid-size insurers, TPAs, MGAs, and brokers who file for clients. Buyer: head of claims. User: first-line handlers. Privacy opens the door: health and vehicle documents hold identity data many teams will not send to another vendor.

Why on-device wins on cost

Every scan Gemini Nano handles costs no inference. Cloud runs only on fallback, so margin grows with Nano coverage. "Your claimants' documents are never uploaded" is also easy to prove in a security review.

What is honest today

Measured: on 24 synthetic documents (clean, other font, degraded) the cloud path read all six checked fields right on 21 of 24; the 3 misses were an empty patient name, never a wrong value. Synthetic and easier than real photos; Gemini Nano is not measured.

A working web prototype, not a claims system. Rules are generic examples, not a real product's wording. No insurer has piloted it, there are no accounts, and accuracy on crumpled, handwritten or non-Latin documents is untested. It flags problems for a human. It does not detect fraud and never decides a claim. Next: per-insurer rule sets, a Chrome extension for e-documents, then Android on the same Firebase AI Logic code.
